Question
DECISION: For ShiftMate, choose multilingual DistilBERT + ONNX versus rules + character/word TF-IDF + logistic regression for offline English/Hindi intent classification, and a PyTorch feed-forward neural network versus the domain baseline + Ridge for task-duration correction. CONTEXT: Android-first and offline-first; Vosk STT; narrow safety-adjacent intents with read-back confirmation; cold start under 5 s; voice command under 2 s. The duration dataset is about 2,500 formula-derived synthetic rows. Real bilingual utterance counts, real task histories, and target-device benchmarks are unknown. STAKES: hackathon delivery, APK/device performance, bilingual robustness, estimator credibility, explainability, and avoiding field claims from synthetic evidence. OPTIONS: advanced/advanced, simple/simple, or independently benchmark-gated challengers.
Pre-council bias scanCompeting anchors were detected: sunk cost and status-quo bias favor the fully specified simple stack, while confirmation bias favors the earlier advanced-stack preference. Planning and narrative fallacies can also confuse sophistication with field robustness. These are signals, not verdicts.
Council Verdict

Where the council agrees

  • Red Team, First Principles, Outsider, and Executor: do not adopt the neural duration estimator from formula-generated synthetic records.
  • All advisors: decide intent classification and duration estimation independently.
  • Expansionist, First Principles, and both Chairmen: character n-gram TF-IDF is a stronger immediate baseline than word-only TF-IDF for transliteration and Vosk noise.
  • All advisors: advanced models should remain replaceable challengers behind versioned interfaces and must clear target-device and real-data gates.

Where the council clashes

The Prosecutor argued that sparse intent models can fail on Hindi morphology, Romanized Hindi, code-switching, and ASR corruption, and that users may rubber-stamp confirmations. The Defender conceded this but showed that these risks justify realistic evaluation, selective acceptance, and explicit fallbacks rather than immediate promotion of an unbenchmarked transformer.

Blind spots

Template and speaker leakage can invalidate comparisons. The project also needs fixed promotion thresholds, a bilingual clarification/manual-entry fallback, privacy-safe logging of abstentions and corrections, and end-to-end testing through Vosk and confirmation rather than clean-text classifier tests alone.

Recommendation

Ship deterministic safety rules plus character n-gram TF-IDF/logistic regression for intent, with abstention, clarification, top-three fallback, and confirmation. Ship the domain formula with Ridge or a constrained additive/GAM residual correction; do not make the PyTorch network the production default. Implement quantized DistilBERT+ONNX as a mandatory same-milestone shadow challenger, and keep the FNN as a duration challenger until real longitudinal evidence exists.

One thing to do first

Within 48 hours, freeze the paired benchmark and collect the first consented English, Hindi, Romanized-Hindi, and code-switched Vosk utterances on the actual target Android device.

Dissent Ledger
  • Make DistilBERT+ONNX a mandatory same-milestone shadow challenger so the baseline does not become permanent through inertia.
  • High-confidence wrong intents remain dangerous despite confirmation because users may rubber-stamp prompts.
  • Log abstentions, alternatives, corrections, and manual overrides to prevent data-path bias.
  • Freeze promotion thresholds before training so results cannot move the goalposts.
